# Phase 0 — sharpened brief (working state, not deliverable)

## 1. Function, not form

Form in the brief: "stop people leaving." That form caps the answer at "make them stay."

Function: **a company needs continuous access to accumulated system-specific judgement —
the knowledge of why this thing is built the way it is — and today that access is bolted to
the continuous employment of a small number of named individuals. The bolt shears at ~18
months.**

Two sub-functions fall out, and they are different problems:
- (a) keep the person applying that judgement here
- (b) stop the judgement leaving with the person

## 2. Banned vocabulary (Phase 1)

retention / retain, compensation / comp / pay / salary / equity, engineer, senior,
culture, career, growth, mentorship, attrition / churn / turnover, loyalty, perks,
promotion, ladder

## 3. The obvious answer (baseline — measure everything against this)

Build a staff+/principal individual track so people can advance without managing; hand out
refresh grants at the 18-month mark; put them on greenfield and architecture ownership;
train the managers; run stay interviews; rotate them to new teams; fund conferences and
learning. Competent, correct, and already what everyone does.

## 4. Adversarial attributes injected

- ...for someone who has already privately decided to go and will be polite in every
  conversation you are able to have with them.
- ...against a rival with 50x the budget who will always win on money, title and logo, and
  who targets your people at precisely this point in their tenure.
- ...where headcount is falling, you cannot add people, and whatever you build has to keep
  working after the person who invented it leaves.

## 5. Ambiguity (unresolvable without asking; stated in the answer)

Two readings, different answers:
- **Pull**: nothing is wrong here; a bigger org simply outbids on scale, prestige and
  optionality, and 18 months is when someone becomes legible to that market.
- **Push**: the work itself changes shape at 18 months — the interesting build phase ends,
  ownership calcifies, and the job becomes custodial.
- **Third, cheap to check**: the 18-month clock is an artefact of a vesting/cliff schedule
  or a cohort hiring spike, not of the work at all.

Reading picked: **push-dominant with a pull trigger** — a bigger offer converts a
pre-existing drift, it does not create one. Stated in the answer with what changes under
the others.

## 6. Retrieved neighbours (deep-mode research) — difference constraint

Generators must produce none of these:

1. Staff/principal individual contributor track parallel to management
2. Refresh grants or off-cycle top-ups timed to the 18-month mark
3. Stay interviews, skip-levels, pulse surveys, exit-interview programmes
4. Manager quality training / fixing the first-time manager problem
5. Internal mobility marketplace — move team every 18-24 months
6. Greenfield rotation, 20% time, hack weeks, innovation days
7. Hoffman's "tour of duty" / The Alliance: explicit 2-4 year mission with a defined end
   and an employability promise
8. Alumni network + boomerang rehire programme, celebrating exits
9. Sabbaticals at tenure milestones
10. Conference speaking, open-source time, external visibility budgets, publishing
11. Learning budgets, certifications, formal mentorship pairings
12. Autonomy over technical decisions, RFC processes, architecture ownership
13. Remote/flexible/four-day week
14. Retention bonuses, extended option exercise windows, longer vesting
15. Mission/purpose framing, connecting work to customer outcomes
16. Developer-experience investment, paying down toil, fixing on-call

## 7. Lenses selected (6 blind generators)

first principles / inversion / actor reversal / constraint extremity / analogical transfer /
biomimicry (deep mode only, mechanism grounding mandatory)
