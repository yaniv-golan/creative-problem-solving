### 1. Assign a persistent, exclusive domain, client, or system to own

Like cleaner wrasse who hold fixed reef stations that client fish return to repeatedly, assign each senior engineer a standing internal 'client' unit whose problems route only through them.

*Checked — [ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3123342/)*

*Note — The biology is confirmed, with a wrinkle the analogy leans on: the literature distinguishes 'resident' clients, which are territorial and depend on one local station, from 'visitor' clients, which are large-bodied and have access to several stations and choose among cleaners. So exclusive routing describes only part of the natural system; the higher-value clients specifically retain partner choice, and partner switching is one of the control mechanisms that keeps cleaners honest.*

It changes what the seat is rather than what it costs, and drawing a permanent ownership boundary is something an engineering manager can do this quarter without asking anyone.

- **What has to be true:** There is a domain narrow enough to be one person's and consequential enough that owning it is real authority — and the company will accept the concentration that follows.
- **Failure mode / cost:** A bus factor of one, and an owner who becomes a bottleneck on everyone else's work; the cleaner-wrasse literature carries the same warning in reverse — the highest-value clients keep the right to switch stations, so the internal counterparty needs an escape route or the arrangement turns into a toll booth.
- **Who runs it:** Whoever controls team and system boundaries — a head of engineering, or an EM with a charter they can sign.

- Set up a standing mandate with no end date and no re-approval cycle: the engineer owns the domain until they explicitly hand it off.
- Mirror Florida scrub-jay cooperative breeding, where helpers stay because a bounded breeding territory awaits them: grant the engineer an actual bounded product territory they alone architect, not a title.
- Copy the fig wasp, obligately paired one species to one fig tree: pair each senior engineer exclusively to one irreplaceable client or system not interchangeable with any other engineer.
- Give the engineer sole sign-off authority over one production system's architecture for a full year, with no committee override, recorded in a signed charter.
- Assign the engineer a single named external client account that persists across projects, so their standing with that client outlasts any internal reorg.
