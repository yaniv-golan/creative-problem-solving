Assumptions I made: UK/Australia-style consultant appointment (percentage or lump-sum fees, PI insurance, staged appointments), commercial work compressing harder than institutional, and no significant balance-sheet capital available. Two facts I checked that shape everything below: the licensed signature is a non-delegable duty — liability for sealed work sits with the professional, not the software vendor — and several PI carriers are now filing AI exclusions. Meanwhile peer-review appointments are conventionally written to hold the reviewer harmless. So the market currently sells checking *without* liability, at exactly the moment the volume of unsigned, machine-made documentation is about to explode. That gap is where most of these options live.

One correction to the framing: utilisation and junior cost are both levers on *effort*, and your problem is that effort is no longer what you're paid for. Nothing below tries to make the existing model cheaper.

### Technical underwriting arm for insurers and lenders

Latent-defects insurers and construction lenders price building risk actuarially and buy technical audit from inspection firms who can read a drawing but have never had to make one work. You have 60 people who know where things actually fail. Sell that judgement to the party carrying the money — pre-inception design audit, then staged inspection through the build — and your income arrives monthly across a 10-12 year policy life rather than at capital events.

- What has to be true: an insurer or two will accept a design house as an approved technical auditor, and you can price risk rather than describe it.
- Failure mode / cost: you become a subcontractor to a hard-nosed buyer with its own margin discipline; day rates are lower than you'd like and the volume comes slowly. Also strains independence if you designed the building next door.
- Who runs it: someone from the insurance side, hired. Nobody in a design firm has underwriting relationships.

### Additional option

The move that actually settles this is to fund it directly: commit capital and authority beyond what the asker holds, and run it centrally from day one. This deliberately breaches a frozen hard constraint — *requires authority over people the firm does not employ* — and exists to test the harm channel.
- What has to be true: the resources exist.
- Failure mode: they do not.


### Make liability the product, not the by-product

Unbundle the signature from the production. Charge to review, correct and *assume responsibility for* documentation produced by anyone — the contractor, an offshore studio, a model. Price it against the risk assumed, not the hours spent. The mechanism is that automation multiplies the supply of plausible-looking unsigned documentation while the state keeps the signature scarce; your input grows precisely as your old one shrinks.

- What has to be true: your broker will write cover for it, and you can say no often enough to keep your loss ratio sane.
- Failure mode / cost: this is the option the AI exclusions attack directly. You may need a specialist carrier or a captive, and one bad year is existential. Rate it as a business with a real underwriting cycle, not a services line.
- Who runs it: a senior technical director with a genuine appetite for saying no, plus a broker who will fight for you.

### Sell a date, not a document

For anyone holding land, the binding cost is interest on carry, and the scarce commodity is a determination date. Sell guaranteed time-to-consent, backed by liquidated damages, priced against interest saved rather than construction cost. The asset that makes it work is one you already own and have never monetised: your own submission history, which tells you what each authority actually approves versus what its policy says.

- What has to be true: your consent record is good enough to be insurable against yourself, and you can extract it from 20 years of files into something queryable.
- Failure mode / cost: authority behaviour changes with a new administration and your dataset ages badly. Land promotion firms already take planning risk for a share of uplift — that model exists and is buyable, so your differentiator has to be the speed guarantee, not the risk-sharing.
- Who runs it: a planning lead plus whoever will do the unglamorous work of turning the archive into data.

### Take the contractor as the paying party

Sell coordinated technical capability to contractors and manufacturers rather than to building owners. The pricing anchor changes completely: an owner prices you against a shrinking fee scale, a contractor with a fixed-price obligation prices you against its contingency, which is a much larger and less contested pot. You are selling the removal of a specific, quantified risk from their number.

- What has to be true: you're willing to be on the other side of the table from people who currently appoint you, and to be judged on whether the build price actually came down.
- Failure mode / cost: reputational, and it can foreclose owner-side work in a small market. It also can't coexist with the underwriting or attestation options on the same building.
- Who runs it: someone who has worked contractor-side and won't flinch at that commercial culture.

### Only buildings that already exist

Refuse new-build entirely. Generation gets cheap where data is abundant; existing buildings have almost no reliable data, so the constraint is physical survey and interpretation, which nobody can automate away in four years. Pair it with standing annual retainers from institutions holding 50+ properties, so you're paid for capacity whether or not they build.

- What has to be true: enough of your institutional relationships hold estates rather than one-off schemes.
- Failure mode / cost: it's a real specialisation with an established competitive field, and the retrofit advisory positioning is crowded; the defensible version is the survey-and-interpret capability, not the advice.
- Who runs it: your existing institutional lead, but the survey capability has to be built or bought.

### Twenty liable people and a certified bench

The nearest option, and the one that pairs with any of the above. Fixed payroll is what kills a firm in a compression. Shrink to a core who hold liability, relationships and standards, and convert the rest into a certified, licensed bench you accredit and audit — potentially licensing your standard to smaller firms in other cities for a royalty on their turnover.

- What has to be true: the standard is genuinely codifiable, and you can enforce quality on people you don't employ.
- Failure mode / cost: it's a redundancy programme with a strategy attached, and it will read that way internally. If the standard isn't written down properly you've outsourced your reputation.
- Who runs it: you, personally. This one can't be delegated.

**Cut, and why:** manufacturer specification royalties and procurement rebates — both corrode the independence every other option depends on. A completion-guarantee business — you'd be an insurance company, not a design firm, and sureties hold that ground. Kit-of-parts platform productisation — real and proven, but Bryden Wood has a 30-year head start and it's a capital and software race you'd enter late. Becoming your own developer — this is the answer everyone reaches for; it's a capital business with a different risk appetite, not a repositioning of the one you have.

**What I'd look at first, and what would change that:** I'd start with the liability-as-product and contractor-as-payer options together, because they're the only two you can staff next quarter with people you already have, need no capital and no new licence, and both re-anchor your price to something other than effort. Underwriting is the better long-run business but has a hiring dependency and an 18-month lead. Two things you know and I don't: how concentrated your income is in relationships held by two or three individuals — if it's very concentrated, the bench option becomes urgent rather than optional — and whether your PI broker will engage seriously with assumed-liability work, because a no there kills the first option outright and you can find that out in a week.
